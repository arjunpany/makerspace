CareerFinder

Built a data-driven career exploration app for low-income students, placing 85th and in the top 5% at Empower Hacks 2.0 among 1,800+ participants.

Timeframe
July 26 – August 2, 2024 (Empower Hacks 2.0)
Tools used
Career dataset/database, data filtering and search logic, web development tools
Category
Web Development, Data
Result
85th place, top 5% of 1,800+ participants

The problem

Empower Hacks 2.0 focused on building solutions for first-generation and low-income students. My teammate and I both come from immigrant families from India that experienced financial challenges, and we saw how education and career opportunities helped our parents create greater financial stability for their families.

We wanted to help students make informed career decisions earlier in life. Low-income students may have less access to career guidance and resources, making it harder to identify careers that fit both their interests and financial goals. CareerFinder was designed to make career data more accessible and help students discover paths that could provide long-term financial opportunities.

How it works

CareerFinder uses a dataset containing information about different career fields, including annual salary, unemployment rate, and women's representation.

Users enter up to three broad fields they're interested in, such as engineering or business, along with the minimum annual salary they would want from a future career. The application filters the dataset using these preferences and recommends specific career fields that match their interests and salary requirements.

After choosing a recommended career, users can view its salary, unemployment rate, and women's representation, giving them additional information to compare potential career paths.

Process

  • Worked with my teammate to identify a problem aligned with the hackathon's focus on first-generation and low-income students.
  • Chose to focus on long-term economic opportunity through career exploration.
  • Used career data containing salary, unemployment, and gender-representation statistics.
  • Designed an input system for users to enter their interests and desired minimum salary.
  • Built the filtering logic to match those preferences with relevant careers.
  • Created the results experience to display detailed statistics for each career.
  • Completed, demonstrated, and submitted CareerFinder during the week-long hackathon.

Results

CareerFinder earned 85th place at Empower Hacks 2.0, finishing in the top 5% among more than 1,800 participants. We successfully completed and demonstrated a working application that turned career and employment data into personalized recommendations for students.

What I'd do differently

I would expand CareerFinder to consider factors beyond salary and general interests, including required education, projected job growth, location, tuition costs, skills, and work environment. I would also improve the recommendation system to rank careers based on how closely they match a user's overall preferences and use a larger, regularly updated dataset.

Eventually, CareerFinder could develop from a career-search tool into a more complete platform that guides students from career discovery → education requirements → training or college options → potential career paths.
