Dual-Axis Solar Tracker

An Arduino-driven mount that follows the sun on two axes to boost panel output by roughly 30% over a fixed mount.

Timeframe
March 2026 – April 2026
Tools used
Arduino Uno, LDR sensors, servo motors, 3D-printed mount, Fusion 360
Category
Electronics, 3D Printing

The problem

Fixed solar panels lose a significant amount of potential output because they only face the sun directly for a moment each day. I wanted to test how much a simple tracking mount could improve real-world output using cheap, off-the-shelf parts.

How it works

Four light-dependent resistors (LDRs) are arranged in a cross pattern behind a divider. An Arduino compares the light readings from each pair and drives two servos — one for tilt, one for pan — to rotate the panel toward the strongest light source. The frame and gearing were designed in Fusion 360 and 3D-printed in PETG for outdoor durability.

Build process

  • Prototyped the LDR sensor logic on a breadboard and tested response curves indoors.
  • Designed and iterated the two-axis gimbal mount in CAD (three print revisions).
  • Wrote the Arduino control loop with dead-zone smoothing to stop servo jitter.
  • Ran a 5-day side-by-side test against a fixed panel, logging voltage every 10 minutes.

Results

The tracking panel produced about 29% more energy over the test period compared to the fixed control panel, with the biggest gains in the early morning and late afternoon.

What I'd do differently

Add a rain/wind sensor to park the panel flat in bad weather, and switch to a weatherproof gearbox — the 3D-printed gears wore down faster than expected after two weeks outdoors.
