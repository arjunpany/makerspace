AUREUS — FRC Team 1318 Competition Robot

Contributed to the electrical assembly and integration of AUREUS, Team 1318's competition robot for the 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition season.

I worked as part of Team 1318's electrical subteam, collaborating with students across electrical, mechanical, and programming to build and maintain the team's 2026 competition robot.

The challenge

The 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition game, REBUILT, challenged alliances to collect and score FUEL while navigating the field and climbing the TOWER. AUREUS needed to combine a fast drivetrain with reliable intake, game-piece handling, shooting, climbing, sensing, and automated control.

Your contribution

As a member of the electrical subteam, I contributed to the assembly, wiring, and electrical integration of AUREUS. I worked alongside other electrical team members to connect and organize the robot's power distribution, motor controllers, sensors, CAN-connected devices, and subsystem wiring.

I also contributed to routing, securing, checking, and troubleshooting electrical connections as the robot's mechanical systems changed throughout the build season. My work was one part of a larger collaborative electrical effort rather than an individually designed system.

How the robot works

AUREUS was built around a four-module swerve drivetrain, with separate drive and steering motors and absolute encoders on each module. Battery power was distributed through a REV Power Distribution Hub (PDH) to the drivetrain and mechanisms, while CAN-based communication connected controllers and sensors throughout the robot.

The electrical system supported the robot's intake, hopper/spindexer, shooter, adjustable hood, climber, drivetrain, sensors, and vision system. The robot also incorporated an IMU, programmable indicator lighting, and a multi-camera vision setup for sensing and automated control.

Process

The season began with the team analyzing the game and developing the robot's mechanical and electrical architecture. As mechanisms were prototyped and assembled, the electrical subteam integrated the electronics required to power and control them.

I contributed during the assembly and integration phase, helping install, connect, route, and secure electrical components and wiring. As the mechanical and programming systems came together, we tested the robot, diagnosed electrical issues, adjusted wiring as designs changed, and prepared the system for competition.

During competitions, the team continued inspecting, troubleshooting, and maintaining the robot between matches to keep it reliable.

Results

AUREUS competed at three Pacific Northwest events during the 2026 season.

Team 1318 ranked 9th at Bonney Lake and received the Innovation in Control Award, then ranked 8th at Sammamish and received the Quality Award. The team qualified for the Pacific Northwest District Championship, where AUREUS earned Team 1318 a second Innovation in Control Award.

Team 1318 ultimately finished 26th out of 147 teams in the Pacific Northwest district with 129 district points.

What I'd do differently

Working on AUREUS taught me a lot about how a competition robot's electrical system comes together, but I would have liked to take on a larger role in the robot's development. While I contributed as part of the electrical subteam, much of the work was distributed across a large team.

In future projects, I want to take greater ownership of the electrical process, from planning the brainpan and power distribution to wiring, integration, and troubleshooting.
