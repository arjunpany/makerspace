4WD Sensor Robot Car

Built a 4-wheel-drive robot car with onboard sensors and IR remote control at an Amazon-hosted STEM robotics camp.

Timeframe
July 2019
Materials
4WD robot car chassis, onboard control board and wiring, battery pack, sensors, IR remote control
Category
Robotics, Electronics
Context
Built at an Amazon-hosted STEM robotics camp

The build

At a robotics camp hosted at an Amazon building, I built a 4-wheel-drive robot car from a beginner robotics kit — a black chassis riding on four yellow-wheeled gear motors, with a control board, wiring, and a battery pack mounted on top to power and drive it.

How it works

The car is driven with a handheld IR remote control, sending commands to the onboard electronics to steer the four wheels. It also used onboard sensors as part of the kit, giving it more than just basic drive-and-steer control.

Results

By the end of the camp, the car was fully assembled and drivable — responding to the remote and moving around on its own four wheels, which was my introduction to combining electronics, wiring, and control into a working robot.

What I'd do differently

At the time, this was a guided build following a kit — looking back, I'd want to dig further into how the sensors and remote control logic actually worked under the hood instead of just following the assembly steps.
