AI Note-Taking Device

A Raspberry Pi device that records a lecture, transcribes it, and turns it into clean AI-generated study notes with charts and diagrams.

Timeframe
June 2026 – July 2026
Tools used
Raspberry Pi 4, 5″ HDMI touchscreen, ReSpeaker mic, custom 3D-printed case, Python 3 (Tkinter), sounddevice + faster-whisper, Anthropic Claude API, matplotlib
Category
Raspberry Pi, AI/ML, Python

The problem

Taking notes during a fast lecture means you either miss what's being said or miss writing it down. I wanted a dedicated, one-purpose device you press once, that listens to the whole lecture and hands you organized, color-coded study notes afterward — no laptop, no app juggling.

How it works

It's a standalone touchscreen device that boots straight into the app, kiosk-style. You tap Start, it records through the ReSpeaker mic; tap Stop, and it transcribes the audio locally with Whisper, then sends the transcript to Claude, which returns structured study notes — headings, key concepts, definitions, examples, formulas, questions, a summary, and even rendered bar/flow charts. Notes are color-coded on screen (blue headings, red facts, green examples, yellow definitions), scrollable by finger, and can be saved, emailed, or deleted — all from the touchscreen, including typing on an on-screen keyboard and connecting to WiFi.

Build process

  • Built the Python/Tkinter UI with a Start/Stop record flow.
  • Added audio capture (ReSpeaker) and on-device Whisper transcription.
  • Integrated the Claude API to turn transcripts into structured notes.
  • Added color-coded rendering plus matplotlib charts and diagrams.
  • Built the saved-notes library: save, email (SMTP), delete, on-screen keyboard, in-app WiFi.
  • Set up kiosk auto-launch on boot.
  • Designed a custom 3D-printable case (parametric OpenSCAD) sized to the exact boards.

Results

A working desk device that goes from spoken lecture to polished, color-coded study notes in well under a minute for short recordings. It saves notes locally, emails them, runs fullscreen on boot, and lives in a custom-printed black case with mounts for the Pi, display, and mic.

What I'd do differently

  • Move transcription to a cloud service (Groq) so long lectures process in seconds instead of minutes.
  • Add a wireless power option so it isn't plug/power dependent.
  • Add an interactive tool so students can ask the AI questions.
  • Maybe cloud sync so notes appear on your phone automatically.
