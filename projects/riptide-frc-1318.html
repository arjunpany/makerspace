Riptide — FRC Team 1318 Competition Robot

Joined Team 1318's electrical subteam as a rookie, learning FRC robot systems while the team developed Riptide for the 2025 REEFSCAPE season.

2025 was my first season with Issaquah Robotics Society. I joined the electrical subteam but spent much of my rookie season observing and learning from experienced members rather than taking responsibility for major parts of the robot.

The challenge

The 2025 FIRST Robotics Competition game, REEFSCAPE, challenged alliances to manipulate two game pieces: CORAL and ALGAE. Robots placed CORAL onto different levels of the REEF, removed and scored ALGAE, and could finish matches by climbing onto CAGES suspended from the BARGE.

Riptide was designed to perform these tasks through a combination of game-piece manipulation, precise positioning, automated control, and climbing.

Your contribution

My role during Riptide's development was primarily learning and observation. As a rookie member of the electrical subteam, I learned from experienced members as they worked on the robot's electrical systems and saw how power distribution, motor controllers, sensors, wiring, mechanical mechanisms, and programming came together in a competition robot.

Rather than owning a particular subsystem, I used the season to develop my understanding of robot construction, electrical integration, testing, troubleshooting, and competition preparation.

The experience gave me a foundation in FRC electrical systems that allowed me to become a more active contributor in the following season.

How the robot works

Riptide was designed around manipulating both CORAL and ALGAE while completing a deep CAGE climb.

A continuous, belt-driven two-stage elevator allowed the robot to reach all four CORAL scoring levels. A powered realignment system used rollers and beam-break sensors to detect and reposition CORAL before scoring.

Riptide's "Owl Head" CORAL outtake used a curved rack-and-pinion mechanism to adjust its yaw angle, allowing fine scoring adjustments without requiring the entire drivetrain to reposition.

The robot also incorporated an ALGAE intake, passive "Alien" funnel for guiding CORAL, a deep CAGE climber, sensors for game-piece detection, and autonomous control systems.

Process

The season began with the team analyzing REEFSCAPE and determining how the robot would acquire, manipulate, and score the game's different pieces. Concepts were prototyped and developed into Riptide's drivetrain, elevator, Owl Head, ALGAE mechanism, funnel, climber, and supporting systems.

As a rookie electrical member, my experience focused on learning how this engineering process worked. I observed how electrical components were incorporated into mechanical systems, how electrical and programming members worked together, and how the robot evolved through integration and testing.

I also saw how problems discovered during testing and competition had to be diagnosed and corrected quickly, giving me my first experience with the development cycle of a full competition robot.

Results

Riptide had a strong 2025 competition season.

Team 1318 ranked 4th at Glacier Peak, reached the finals at SunDome and received the Autonomous Award, and ranked 5th at Auburn, where the team received the Creativity Award.

At the Pacific Northwest District Championship, Riptide went 10–2 in qualification matches, ranked 4th of 50 teams, and helped Team 1318 finish the district season 11th of 147 teams, qualifying for the FIRST Championship.

At the 2025 FIRST Championship in Houston, Team 1318 competed in the Milstein Division. Riptide was selected by the #1 alliance and advanced to the Milstein Division Finals, finishing the season as a Championship Division Finalist.

What I'd do differently

Looking back, I would have tried to become more involved earlier in my rookie season. I spent much of the year observing experienced members and learning how the team operated, but the experience made me want to move beyond observation and take a more active role in building the robot.

The following season, I became more involved with the electrical subteam and contributed directly to the electrical assembly and integration of AUREUS.
